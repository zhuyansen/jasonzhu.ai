If you want to learn about investing, here's my recommended reading order:

## 1️⃣ Stage One: Building a Foundation

1. *Mia and the Magic Money Tree (Kiyosaki's Little Dog Money)*: Financial concepts explained through a fairy tale
2. *The Accounting Game*: Learn accounting through the lens of a lemonade stand

## 2️⃣ Stage Two: Getting Started with Index Funds

1. *Stay the Course* by John Bogle, the father of index funds: Understand the wisdom behind passive index investing

## 3️⃣ Stage Three: Investing in Individual Stocks (Most people won't need to go this far)

1. *The Essays of Warren Buffett*: An introduction to value investing
2. *The Little Book That Builds Wealth* / financial statement analysis: Learn how to value a company through its financials so you can spot undervalued opportunities — financial statements are full of traps, so tread carefully

## 4️⃣ Stage Four: Political Science & Economics

**How does society work?**

1. *The End of Poverty* by Jeffrey Sachs: A deep dive into global poverty, economic development, and international cooperation
2. *The Low-Desire Society* by Kenichi Ohmae: An in-depth look at Japan's culture of low ambition and declining consumption
3. *The Low-IQ Society* by Kenichi Ohmae: Examines the phenomenon of collective intellectual decline in Japan
4. *The M-Shaped Society* by Kenichi Ohmae: How prolonged economic stagnation polarizes income classes — the middle class hollows out while the lower and upper ends grow
5. *Professional* by Kenichi Ohmae: In an era of uncertainty, becoming a true expert is the only reliable strategy
6. *The Art of Thinking* by Kenichi Ohmae: In the new age, the gap between people will be largely determined by the quality of their thinking — sharpen your logical reasoning
7. *Ohmae on AI and Fintech*: How AI is poised to transform the business landscape
8. *半拿铁's "A History of the Internet"*: Learning from history — what the rise of mobile internet can tell us about the trajectory of AI

**What about politics?**

9. *A Biased Look at Politics*: Wherever there are people, there are grudges; wherever there are grudges, there are power games. Politics is the arena — political science is your defense against the dark arts.

**What about economics?**

10. *Witnessing the Counter-Current* by Fu Peng: Build a coherent worldview of the global economy

**Future predictions and how to position yourself?**

11. *The Global Age* by Kenichi Ohmae: The world stage has no borders
12. *The Sovereign Individual*: How individuals in the information age can leverage technology to achieve financial independence, transcend geographic constraints, and protect their wealth and freedom
13. *The Balaji Predictions — Technology, Truth, and a Guide to Building the Future*: Technology is the driving force of history — it sits upstream of culture, and upstream of politics. Truth is rarely popular, and what's popular is rarely true; learning to distinguish one from the other is the first step. To get lucky, you have to learn to seize opportunities — don't argue, build.

## Supplement: Money & Currency

1. *The Bitcoin Standard* (or *The Future of Money*): Traces the evolution of money — from primitive barter to metal coins, the gold standard, government-issued fiat, and finally digital currency. What does it mean for money to be "sound"?

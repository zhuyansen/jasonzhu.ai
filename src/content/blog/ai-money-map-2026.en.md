A few days ago I saw this joke about AI monetization on X:

> Got GPUs? Sell GPUs. No GPUs? Sell tokens. No tokens? Sell ads. No ads? Sell community access. No community? Sell courses. No courses? Sell custom workflows. No workflows? Sell digital downloads.

Funny, but rough around the edges. I expanded it into a complete map, which got nearly 50,000 views and close to 400 bookmarks. This post is the written version, with the judgments I couldn't fit into a tweet.

## The Seven-Layer AI Business Map

### Upstream: Selling Resources

- Electricity
- Data centers
- GPU / compute leasing
- Model training
- Model APIs
- Token resale

The layer closest to money and the highest barrier to entry. Electricity and data centers are the domain of governments and tech giants. GPU leasing and token resale are still within reach for regular people — but they're fundamentally capital and channel businesses. What matters is your cost basis and payment terms, not your technical skills.

### Midstream: Selling Capabilities

- Agents
- Workflows
- SaaS
- Vertical applications
- Enterprise solutions
- AI outsourcing & consulting
- Custom development

Most people with a technical background are crammed into this layer. The brutal truth: capabilities depreciate fast — the Agent you spent three months building might become a native model feature in six months. The ones who thrive are running in two directions: either going deep into a vertical industry where domain know-how can't be replaced by models, or moving downstream to charge directly for results.

### Downstream: Selling Results

- Help businesses make more money
- Help businesses cut costs
- Help individuals work more efficiently
- Help individuals find jobs
- Help creators grow their audience
- Help executives make decisions

Customers don't care what model or framework you used — they only care whether the metrics moved. This layer has the most pricing power because you're selling the delta, not billable hours. The rise of the FDE (Field Delivery Engineer) role is the signal: people who understand AI go directly into the scenario, deliver results, and share in the upside.

### Traffic Layer: Selling Attention

- Ads
- Social media content
- Newsletters
- Communities
- Affiliate marketing
- Sponsorships

The flip side of the AI content explosion is that attention is scarcer than ever. This layer has the lowest startup cost — one person, one account, and you're in — but it requires compound interest over time. Account authority, subscriber lists, community trust all accumulate. Its greatest value isn't actually ad revenue — it's supplying cheap distribution to your other businesses. Once you have traffic, you gain a low-cost customer acquisition channel for any of the other six layers.

### Education Layer: Selling Knowledge

- Courses
- Training
- Consulting
- Coaching / guided programs
- Corporate training

Information asymmetry always exists; only the topics change. What's unique about the AI era is how fast knowledge depreciates — last year's course needs a rewrite this year. That's why the education businesses that survive are shifting from "sell a course once" to "ongoing guided programs": memberships, community-based learning, quarterly content updates. They're selling continuous follow-up, not a set of recorded videos.

### Emotional Layer: Selling Companionship

- AI therapy / counseling
- AI chat companions
- AI companionship products
- AI emotional products

The layer most underestimated by technical people. Character.AI-style products crush productivity tools in time spent per user. Productivity products save users time; companionship products make users willingly spend time — and the latter often has better retention and willingness to pay. Of course, this layer also carries the highest compliance and ethical risks.

### Asset Layer: Selling Reproducible Products

- Prompts
- Skills
- MCP Servers
- Workflow templates
- Agent templates
- Datasets
- Digital goods
- Open source commercialization

The layer with marginal costs closest to zero: build once, sell forever. Skills, MCP Servers, and Agent templates are 2026's new categories — the niches are still empty, standards are just solidifying, and now is the time to claim territory. Open source commercialization is the advanced play: use open source to build trust and traffic, then monetize through enterprise editions and managed hosting.

## Every AI Business Comes Down to Five Transactions

Compress the seven-layer map further and you'll find every AI business ultimately falls into five transaction types:

1. **Selling resources**: electricity, GPUs, tokens
2. **Selling tools**: models, SaaS, Agents
3. **Selling services**: consulting, delivery, customization
4. **Selling traffic**: ads, media, communities
5. **Selling trust**: brands, education, companionship

AI is just a new production tool. The logic of making money has barely changed.

The biggest shift is that more and more industries are **moving from selling time to selling automation, selling results, and selling trust**.

## How Regular People Should Pick Their Position

Three practical guidelines:

**First, choose from the bottom up, not top down.** Upstream requires capital. Midstream requires technical skill but depreciates fast. Downstream, traffic, education, and assets are where regular people belong — low startup costs, and they all compound on trust.

**Second, the traffic layer is an amplifier for every other business.** No matter which layer you ultimately operate in, start by building an account that produces consistently (X, a newsletter, a blog — anything works). It won't make big money directly, but it drives your customer acquisition cost for everything else toward zero.

**Third, convert one-time transactions into recurring ones.** Turn a course into a coaching program. Turn templates into subscriptions. Turn project delivery into revenue sharing on results. In the AI era, both knowledge and tools depreciate too fast for one-time purchases to sustain a long-term business. Recurring revenue is the real moat.

## FAQ

### What's the lowest-cost entry point for regular people to monetize AI in 2026?

The traffic layer and the asset layer. The former requires just an account and the ability to ship content consistently. The latter (Prompts, Skills, Agent templates, MCP Servers) costs you effort once and sells infinitely, with marginal cost near zero. The two amplify each other: your account distributes your digital assets. That's the combination that's working best right now.

### Should someone with a technical background build SaaS or do delivery?

In 2026, the balance clearly tilts toward delivery (selling results). General-purpose SaaS faces constant pressure from models adding native features, while deep industry-specific delivery — like the FDE model — can't be replaced by models and commands higher pricing power. If you insist on building a product, pick a vertical industry and tie it to specific outcome metrics. Don't build a general-purpose tool.

### What's the fundamental difference between making money with AI versus the old internet?

The transaction structure hasn't changed (still resources, tools, services, traffic, and trust). What changed is the delivery method: from selling time to selling automation and results. Previously, one person's output had a ceiling. Now AI expands individual capacity by one to two orders of magnitude, making "a small team eating the lunch of what used to require a whole company" a genuinely common possibility for the first time.

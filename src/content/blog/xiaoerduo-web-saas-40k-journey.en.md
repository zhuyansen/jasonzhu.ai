## Key Lessons

### 1. Demand Discovery Is Your Biggest Weakness — and Your Biggest Lever

A common trap for indie developers (including the author in the early days) is **building the product first, then searching for users**. The order should be exactly the opposite.

Core methods for uncovering real demand:
- **Observe genuine pain points among overseas users**: Reddit, Quora, and Product Hunt discussions
- **Analyze competitors' weaknesses**: find the gaps they haven't filled and move in
- **Validate willingness to pay**: figure out how much users would actually spend to solve the problem
- **Start small**: don't try to build a massive platform out of the gate

### 2. Traffic Acquisition Demands Speed and Total Coverage

The author repeatedly hammers on the importance of moving fast and leaving no channel unexplored:

> *"When it comes to traffic, speed wins everything — go everywhere and leave no stone unturned!"

**SEO Strategy:**
- Launch fast to claim keyword territory before competitors
- Aggressively build backlinks — speed matters here too
- Competitors are your best teachers: study where their traffic comes from and reverse-engineer their link-building
- Lead with content: even before the product is fully polished, start laying the content groundwork

**Paid Advertising:**
- Google Ads is the core paid channel for global SaaS
- Start with a small budget to test, then scale up what works
- The author admits, "I still regret not starting with Google Ads at the highest tier from day one"
- When paid ads are performing well, it genuinely feels like a money-printing machine

### 3. Never Neglect Mobile

The author drives this point home with emphasis:

> *"Take mobile seriously. Take mobile seriously. Take mobile seriously."

Unless your product is strictly B2B, the mobile experience directly impacts conversion rates and user retention. For a large share of users worldwide, a smartphone is their primary — sometimes only — way to access the internet.

### 4. Your Product Is a Global Vending Machine

The most compelling thing about global SaaS is this:

> *"You're not just playing the role of creator — the worldwide vending machine you've built keeps generating revenue for you 24 hours a day."

This is exactly why people building global SaaS products are notorious for not going to bed early — because it feels too good. Watching users across different time zones pay automatically becomes genuinely addictive.

### 5. Product Marketing Is Evolving in the AI Era

The author has noticed a clear shift in how product updates get promoted:

- It's no longer just a simple changelog
- It's about crafting platform-specific content and distributing it across social channels
- AI is blurring the traditional lines between marketing disciplines
- Content marketing and product marketing are increasingly merging into one

### 6. The Most Focused People Win Hardest in the Age of AI

> *"The smarter AI gets, the more productive people with clear goals become. At first it's just workflow and efficiency gains — but then the cognitive gap between them and everyone else starts to widen, and that's where quantity turns into quality. The gap becomes exponential."

AI tools amplify the efficiency of people who are already pointed in the right direction — they don't replace the thinking.

## The Turning Point: From Failure to Success

The author's path was anything but smooth:
- Ran a WeChat public account — learned content creation
- Built an iOS app — learned product development
- Joined the Shengcai Youshu community — expanded business perspective
- Eventually pivoted to global web SaaS — found real PMF

Every apparent "failure" was building experience for what came later. The key is **never stop trying**.

## Advice for Newcomers to Global Markets

1. **Demand first**: Spend serious time on demand validation before you write a single line of code
2. **Speed wins**: Move fast on traffic, and lay your SEO foundation early
3. **Learn from competitors**: Your competition is your best teacher — study how they acquire customers
4. **Prioritize mobile**: Overseas users skew heavily toward mobile
5. **Use Google Ads**: Paid advertising is an accelerant for growth
6. **Keep iterating**: Going from your first dollar to $40K/month takes sustained optimization

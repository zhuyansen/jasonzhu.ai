New accounts come with just $5 in free credits that expire in three months — barely enough for any serious product development. OpenAI for Startups is the official credits program: standard applications typically get you $1,500–$2,500, while VC-backed startups can unlock $10K–$100K+, along with higher rate limits and dedicated support. Here's a full breakdown of all three paths I've personally gotten to work.

## Prerequisites Before You Apply

OpenAI's primary channel runs through VC/accelerator referrals. Going in cold has a low success rate and yields smaller credits. Before you start, make sure you can check all three boxes:

- You have a registered company (or are in the process of registering one)
- You have a clearly defined product — ideally with an MVP or real users already
- Your product genuinely requires heavy OpenAI API usage, not just looking for free credits

The application is free and won't affect your existing OpenAI account.

## Path 1: OpenAI for Startups Official Channel

The official channel offers the highest credit amounts, but you must have a referral code to get in. The process has four steps:

**1. Get a Referral Code (the most critical step)**

Reach out to your VC or accelerator. Sequoia, a16z, Thrive, Kleiner Perkins, YC, and others are all official OpenAI partners. Just ask directly:

> Can you give me the OpenAI for Startups referral code?

The format is typically `PARTNER-XXXX-XXXX`.

**2. Open the Application**

Go to the official OpenAI page, click "I'm ready to apply for credits," or scroll to the bottom of the page and click Apply.

**3. Fill Out the Application Form**

The form is short and doesn't require a pitch deck. That said, make sure to nail these fields:

- Referral Code (required)
- Full company name, country of registration, and website
- Product name + one-sentence description
- Detailed use case (this is the most important field)
- Estimated monthly API usage (in tokens)
- Current traction: user count, revenue, growth metrics
- Team information

**4. Wait for a Decision**

Expect a response within 1–4 weeks after submitting. VC-backed applications are usually reviewed faster.

> Key to improving your odds: be as specific as possible about *why your product needs OpenAI* and *what concrete outcomes you'll achieve with the credits*. Vague answers almost always get rejected.

## Path 2: Ramp (Easiest Option — No VC Required)

Ramp is many founders' go-to choice. Approval rates are extremely high and the process is nearly instant.

Steps:

1. Sign up at Ramp's website (free business card)
2. Complete a simple KYC process (company information)
3. Find OpenAI on the Rewards page and click Claim
4. Link your OpenAI account → credits up to $2,500 are automatically applied

There's almost no written application required. This is the most efficient path for early-stage founders.

## Path 3: Microsoft for Startups Founders Hub

This one doesn't give you OpenAI credits directly, but Azure OpenAI runs on exactly the same models — and the credit amounts are solid.

- Sign up at the Microsoft for Startups website
- Select the AI track and submit a brief application
- You'll typically receive $2,000–$5,000 in Azure credits (applicable to OpenAI models)

## How to Write a Strong Application

The OpenAI review team cares most about three things: genuine need, growth potential, and compliant use. Here's a product description template that's worked for me — feel free to adapt it:

**Product description example:**

> We're building an AI-powered SaaS product in [your vertical] with XX paying customers. We currently call GPT-4o/o3 approximately XX million tokens per month, primarily for [specific features: conversational AI / content generation / data analysis / etc.]. These credits will help us ship our next major release, with a target of growing our user base to XX within 3 months.

**Use case example (the more specific, the better):**

- Core feature 1: Users input natural language → o3 model generates a report (estimated XX calls/day)
- Core feature 2: Multi-turn conversation memory (using the Assistants API)
- Expected outcome: After using credits to run A/B testing, we project a XX% improvement in retention

**Traction template:**

> Current MRR: $X/month; Users: XX; Month-over-month growth: XX%; Backed by XX VCs/angel investors.

## After You Receive Credits

- **Expiration**: Typically 12 months; some grants are 6 months
- **Check your balance**: OpenAI Dashboard → Billing → Credits
- **Usage tips**: Use credits for your production environment first; set Usage Limits to avoid going over budget; default to GPT-4o-mini or o3-mini to keep costs down

If your application is rejected, wait three months and try again — or pivot to the Ramp or Microsoft channels in the meantime.

## Tips to Maximize Your Credits

1. **Apply through all three channels simultaneously** — Ramp, Microsoft, and OpenAI's official program don't conflict with each other
2. Join platforms like AI Perks and Founder Stack, which have ready-made application guides and referral resources
3. If you're a YC alum or part of a top accelerator, reach out directly to the OpenAI Startup team — it moves much faster

---

The whole strategy comes down to one thing: present yourself as a founder who is *genuinely building a product*, *genuinely needs OpenAI*, and *has real growth to show for it*. The more specific your application and the more authentic your traction, the more credits you'll get. For founders without VC backing, the recommended path is to start with Ramp to grab the initial $2,500, then take a shot at the official channel.

Every week I count the websites that newly put up checkout links on 8 payment platforms (Stripe, Gumroad, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Creem, Dodo, Paddle, Waffo) to see what people are selling, what is picking up, and which needs nobody has served yet.

## This week's numbers

- The link graph picked up **4,803 new sellers** this week: 918 sell software, and 606 of those are AI products. By platform: Stripe 3,381, Gumroad 976, Lemon Squeezy 195, Polar 148, Dodo 70, Creem 31, Paddle 2. Still no new sellers recorded for Waffo.
- Last week's count has been topped up to 4,741 (the last issue said 4,735), and this week's number will keep rising too.
- Comparing the last four weeks (October 19 to November 15) with the four before: 21,200 new sellers against 17,900. The share selling software is 18.9% against 18.8%, unchanged. Within software, AI products went from 56% to 66%. The share of people selling software is flat, but more and more of the new software sellers are building AI products.
- Dodo had 70 new sellers this week, about twice last week's 36, or 1.5% of the total. Lemon Squeezy was 4.1% and Polar 3.1%. One week does not tell you much; wait for the full November numbers.
- 195 sellers put up their first Lemon Squeezy link this week. Lemon Squeezy announced on November 16 that it is shutting down (see the payment platform news below), so these people have to plan a move just after opening shop.

> Weekly numbers swing a lot: the link graph picks up new links in batches, and we have seen anywhere from 1,100 to 6,500 a week. Look at monthly numbers for trends.

## 5 new products worth a look

1. **Chismozo** (chismozo.com, paid via Lemon Squeezy): a plugin for Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve Studio that edits multicam podcasts. It cuts to whoever is talking, goes to a wide shot when people talk over each other, drops your sponsor and CTA clips into pauses near the times you choose, and lowers the mics nobody is talking into. One-time price of 490 MXN (about $27) for two computers, with a 15-day free trial and no card. The homepage puts a price table right up front comparing it with AutoPod ($29/month) and AutoCut ($19.80/month).
2. **LumeGrid Case Pack** (funeral.lumegrid.ai, paid via Stripe): for funeral homes. Staff fill in one intake (names, dates of birth and death, survivors, service details, 20 to 60 photos and short clips) and get back an obituary in three lengths, a printable 4-page program and a 1080p photo and video tribute. The funeral home approves everything and delivers it to the family under its own brand. The first case is free, then $19 per case or $129/month for unlimited cases. Written pieces within 4 service hours, the tribute within 1 business day.
3. **StillMouse** (stillmouse.com, paid via Stripe): a Mac app for people with shaky hands. It takes the tremor out of the pointer, holds the pointer still while a button is down, and ignores clicks you did not mean. The page cites 9 published studies and says plain smoothing leaves 66% of the shake while StillMouse leaves 7%. 7-day trial with no card or email, then a one-time $29.99 (launch price until the end of the year, normally $34.99). The interface comes in 11 languages.
4. **KenpoAssist** (kenpoassist.skynet-ai.org, paid via Stripe): a Windows app for staff at Japanese health insurance societies. Type in a member's question and it drafts a reply and lists the legal basis, such as the article of the Health Insurance Act. It runs on your own Claude or ChatGPT account, and the inquiry history stays on your PC. One-time license per seat: 1,000 yen through Stripe, or 500 yen if you pay with the JPYC yen stablecoin. 14-day free trial.
5. **BidPulse** (thebidpulse.com, paid via Stripe): a Chrome extension for construction subcontractors. Open a bid on BuildingConnected, click once, and it reads the project name, general contractor, location and due date, writes a follow-up email and opens it in Outlook or Gmail. 10 jobs a month free, Solo $19/month ($15/month billed yearly). PlanHub and other bid platforms are on the way.

## One play this week: live inside software the industry already uses

Three of this week's five do not build their own platform. They sit inside software their users already open every day: Chismozo is a Premiere and DaVinci plugin, BidPulse only shows up on BuildingConnected pages, and KenpoAssist runs on the Claude or ChatGPT subscription the user already pays for. I also saw a Mac app this week, ShipLocal, that translates your App Store listing into all 50 languages and writes it straight back into App Store Connect, Pro at $39.99/year.

- **No need to convince anyone to switch tools**: subcontractors already read bids on BuildingConnected, editors already cut in Premiere. The product is one more button, so getting started costs almost nothing.
- **The host is the acquisition channel**: the host has a clear user base and clear search terms ("Premiere podcast plugin", "BuildingConnected follow up"), so people are easy to find, and you can price directly against similar plugins.
- **Pricing can be very concrete**: per case (LumeGrid, $19 a case), per seat for life (KenpoAssist), or a one-time price set against a competitor's monthly fee (Chismozo). Buyers can do the math at a glance.

The risk is the host. If the host changes its API or its rules, you change with it. Chismozo says plainly on its homepage that DaVinci does not let plugins change volume or move the timeline, so half of its features are missing there. Check what the host actually opens up before you build.

## Payment platform news

- **Lemon Squeezy is shutting down**: on November 16, CEO JR Farr announced on the blog that Lemon Squeezy stopped accepting new store sign-ups that day and **will stop processing payments on March 31, 2027**, a deadline that will not be extended. The reason is that Stripe does not want to run two merchant of record products and will keep only Stripe Managed Payments. Merchants in supported countries can move to Managed Payments, with migration tooling arriving on December 2; merchants elsewhere have to switch providers, and Lemon Squeezy offers a data export and a migration intake form. Source: https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/blog/saying-goodbye-to-lemon-squeezy
- The migration docs say merchants who move get a **1.5%** promotional Managed Payments rate through December 1, 2027, on top of standard Stripe processing fees, starting when you install the migration toolkit. Note that Managed Payments is not a like-for-like replacement: no PayPal for now (PayPal subscribers have to resubscribe), no storefronts, no delivery of files or license keys, no affiliates, checkout only through Stripe Checkout and Payment Links, and subscribers with India-issued cards must reauthorize after the move. Source: https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/migration
- **Dodo Payments**: the same day, Dodo published a post going after Lemon Squeezy migrations, offering hands-on help moving subscriptions and an open-source `dodo-migrate` CLI (products, customers, percentage coupons and active license keys). Standard rates, no discount. Source: https://dodopayments.com/blogs/lemon-squeezy-shutting-down-migrate-to-dodo-payments

If you sell downloads or license keys on Lemon Squeezy, you will need a separate solution for that part after moving to Managed Payments, so it is best to start before mid-January.

Short Answer (Key Takeaways)

Independent software and AI monetization has shifted decisively toward Merchant of Record (MoR) platforms in 2026. While Stripe still leads total overall checkout volume across the broader internet, empirical analysis of roughly 200,000 live checkout links across 198,000 websites shows that software and AI founders disproportionately choose newer MoR providers—specifically Dodo Payments (71% software share, 29% AI share), Polar (58% software, 27% AI), and Creem (65% software, 24% AI)—compared with Stripe (9% software, 7% AI).

Driving that split is global sales-tax compliance and cross-border seller onboarding. Traditional payment processors leave multi-jurisdiction VAT and sales tax calculation to the merchant, whereas MoR platforms act as the legal reseller—a gap that explains why newer entrants expanded rapidly even before Stripe expanded Stripe Managed Payments.

1. Why Checkout-Link Telemetry Tells a Clearer Story Than Surveys

Developer payment preferences are frequently distorted by social-media anecdotes. Founders based in the United States or Western Europe typically default to Stripe because incorporation and banking are straightforward locally, while creators and technical founders in Asia, Latin America, or Eastern Europe face strict entity requirements and cross-border payout blocks.

Observing actual public checkout links embedded on live websites removes self-reporting bias. In JasonZhu.AI's 200,000 checkout links report (jasonzhu.ai/en/blog/indie-payments-report-2026), researchers examined approximately 200,000 sellers across seven major platforms—Stripe, Gumroad, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Creem, Dodo Payments, and Paddle—matching each checkout domain to the date it first appeared alongside homepage classification of what the merchant actually sells.

The dataset captures structural momentum across four consecutive years: 21,000 new sellers recorded in 2023, 29,000 in 2024, 56,000 in 2025, and 82,000 new sellers logged in 2026 before mid-October alone, topping 10,000 new active sellers in both August and September 2026.

2. Software and AI Are Outpacing Traditional Digital Goods

Across the broader creator economy, digital downloads, coaching services, consulting retainers, and local service bookings still make up the majority of payment links. Among 2026's new sellers overall, roughly 38% sell non-digital services or offline offerings, 21% sell educational courses or coaching, and 14% sell ebooks and community memberships.

Inside the software slice, however, growth accelerated dramatically throughout 2026. Throughout 2025, software held steady at roughly 6% to 8% of newly appearing checkout sites. Starting in March 2026, software climbed sharply to reach 20% in September 2026—one in every five newly tracked sellers. Pure AI applications rose even steeper over the same stretch, increasing from 2% of new sellers earlier in the cycle to 12% by late 2026.

4. Key Operational Patterns Behind Indie Seller Growth

Inspecting analytics tags—such as shared Google Analytics, Tag Manager, AdSense, Microsoft Clarity, and Meta pixels—across roughly 17,000 software and AI sellers revealed a striking structural pattern: independent builders increasingly operate multi-site portfolios rather than single flagship startups. Analysis of 276 linked clusters covering 666 domains surfaced four distinct playbooks:

  • Rapid tool factories: Individual builders shipping one single-purpose utility every week or two, hosted across separate domains or free Cloudflare Pages subdomains under a shared analytics property.
  • Localized exact-match utilities: Simple single-feature web tools (such as image editors or file converters) cloned into German, Spanish, Dutch, and Polish keyword domains monetized with display ads and lightweight checkout links.
  • Verticalized SaaS skins: A single core scheduling or AI intake workflow packaged separately for distinct niche trades—hotels, marinas, veterinary clinics, or subcontractors—each behind a vertical-specific brand domain.
  • Multi-country pricing clones: Identical products segmented by country-code domains (.de, .fr, .es, .pl) to localize pricing and capture regional search intent.

5. The Lemon Squeezy Sunset and Merchant of Record Reshuffling

Payment dynamics tightened further in November 2026 when Lemon Squeezy officially announced (lemonsqueezy.com/blog/saying-goodbye-to-lemon-squeezy) that it had stopped accepting new stores and would cease processing payments on March 31, 2027, consolidating operations into Stripe Managed Payments. While Stripe offers migrating merchants a promotional 1.5% Managed Payments rate through December 1, 2027, Managed Payments lacks key indie staples at launch—including built-in license-key delivery, storefronts, affiliate programs, and PayPal checkout support.

That gap immediately triggered aggressive competition among Polar, Dodo Payments, Paddle, and Creem. Builders selling downloadable files or software license keys who operate from jurisdictions ineligible for direct Stripe entities now rely heavily on agile MoR alternatives rather than rebuilding billing compliance from scratch.

6. Checkout Link Abuse and Supply-Chain Warnings

Public payment telemetry also exposes ecosystem abuse. Roughly 17,000 websites in the 200,000-seller dataset shared an identical checkout link across five or more separate domains. While legitimate multi-product creators explain part of that overlap, deeper inspection uncovered paid backlink-network storefronts selling guest posts at scale and compromised websites injected with hidden checkout redirects.

Separating authentic SaaS adoption from automated link-farm spam requires looking beyond raw domain counts—filtering out shared storefront links and inspecting homepage authenticity alongside payout stability before selecting a billing stack.

7. Practical Decision Rules for Choosing a Stack in Late 2026

  1. Standard Stripe (2.9% + 30¢ US): Remains unbeatable on margin when operating through a US LLC or Hong Kong limited company with established banking and sufficient accounting support for regional VAT/sales tax thresholds.
  2. Creem or Dodo Payments: Strongest practical options for solo developers in mainland China or underserved jurisdictions needing immediate global MoR tax handling and transparent local payouts without waiting weeks for foreign company incorporation.
  3. Polar: Ideal for developer-facing tools, API metering, and open-source maintainers operating in Stripe/MoR-supported jurisdictions who value modern usage billing and rapid SDK integration.
  4. Paddle: Best aligned with higher-ACV B2B SaaS teams prioritizing procurement-friendly invoicing, automated dunning, and multi-year compliance stability.

8. Why Regional Tax Thresholds Drive the MoR Shift Faster Than Pricing

Many technical founders initially assume platform pricing alone drives adoption toward Creem, Polar, or Dodo Payments. In practice, administrative compliance overhead creates the tipping point far earlier than a 1% processing spread. Under European Union VAT rules for digital services, US state-level economic nexus thresholds (often triggered by transaction counts as low as 200 sales in certain states), and UK VAT registration demands on cross-border B2C software, a solo engineer shipping a $19/month utility to customers in 35 countries accumulates multi-country filing risk almost overnight.

Because a Merchant of Record legally absorbs invoicing, VAT/GST collection, remittance, and customer chargeback mediation, technical teams reclaim dozens of engineering and bookkeeping hours each quarter. That structural advantage explains why even slightly higher nominal MoR percentages consistently win mindshare among lean indie SaaS portfolios running multiple micro-products simultaneously.

FAQ

Why do so few Stripe sellers appear to build AI products compared with Polar or Dodo?

Stripe serves millions of traditional businesses—consultants, agencies, e-commerce brands, and local services—so software represents just 9% of its massive volume despite Stripe holding the largest absolute count of SaaS merchants (over 103,000 new sellers since 2025). Newer platforms like Polar, Dodo, and Creem were specifically designed around software and AI billing workflows.

Is Stripe Managed Payments a direct replacement for a full Merchant of Record?

For tax calculation and remittance on standard Stripe Checkout and Payment Links, yes—at an additional 3.5% fee above base processing (or 1.5% promotional pricing for eligible Lemon Squeezy migrations). However, developers needing built-in license keys, digital file hosting, storefronts, or affiliate tracking still need supplemental tools or dedicated MoRs.

How can founders verify payment link trends without vendor marketing bias?

Review independent longitudinal studies tracking public checkout URLs—such as the 200,000-seller dataset published by JasonZhu.AI (jasonzhu.ai/en/blog/indie-payments-report-2026) tracking newly added payment links across major providers—and verify published fee tables directly on each provider's documentation page before committing.

2. The Five Verification Rejections That Stall Applications

Reviewing real cross-border rejection logs highlights five exact bottlenecks founders trip over before getting approved:

  1. Submitting utility or card statements instead of ID proof during LLC/agent review: When registering online, use the residential address matching your government ID card directly as your contact address—avoiding mismatched proof-of-address loops later.
  2. Uploading only the front of an ID card: Single-file upload boxes reject cropped front-only scans. Merge both front and back faces clearly onto one single PDF page before uploading.
  3. Receiving an EIN number string without IRS Letter 147C / CP 575: Simply being emailed a nine-digit EIN number by a quick filing contractor is insufficient when Stripe or Mercury asks for official tax verification. Before hiring any filer, confirm in writing that the official IRS confirmation letter PDF (documented on IRS EIN guidance) is delivered.
  4. Submitting domestic national ID cards to US Stripe KYC: International founders activating a US-entity Stripe profile must verify identity using a valid international passport rather than a local national ID card.
  5. Lack of source-of-funds evidence for initial Mercury funding: Late-stage banking compliance checks commonly ask for a bank deposit certificate or statement proving the origin of initial operating capital transferred into Mercury.

3. Upsells to Decline During Registered Agent Checkout

Low headline formation packages routinely mark up checkout totals with optional add-ons solo founders rarely need. Two extras worth declining upfront include proprietary "Business Identity" web/email bundles (when you can launch a clean HTTPS website and branded email on your own domain for ~$10) and $150–$200 "Expedited EIN Filing" upsells—given non-US citizens can either fax Form SS-4 directly to the IRS free of charge or hire a specialized filer for roughly $25 including official IRS confirmation letters.

When completing Mercury banking forms afterward, keep company and personal address fields cleanly separated: enter your Wyoming registered agent address as the company mailing location (mainly governing physical debit-card forwarding) while supplying your exact home residential address matching your ID card for personal KYC verification, paired with an active US or international SMS phone line capable of receiving security codes reliably.

4. Tax Treatment and Year-End Compliance Non-US Founders Cannot Skip

Keeping incorporation lean does not eliminate federal US tax reporting obligations. Because Stripe and Mercury operate inside US financial rails, solo foreign founders should understand two critical distinctions early—and consult a qualified cross-border CPA once revenue scales:

  • W-8BEN & treaty classifications: Understanding whether revenue constitutes remote personal services versus software royalties dictates whether default 30% withholding applies or treaty rates (for example, 10% under specific treaty articles) apply on Stripe tax onboarding forms.
  • Mandatory Form 5472 + Pro-Forma Form 1120: A foreign-owned single-member disregarded US LLC is required to file annual IRS Form 5472 informational returns disclosing owner-entity transactions—even in zero-revenue startup years—with strict $25,000+ statutory penalties for non-filing.

FAQ

Can a non-US resident really form a Wyoming LLC, open Mercury, and activate Stripe under $200?

Yes. Direct formation via a basic Wyoming registered agent (~$143 first year) plus low-cost or DIY EIN filing ($0–$25) brings core entity and tax-ID setup to roughly $168 upfront, while Mercury and Stripe charge zero account-opening fees.

Why is IRS Letter 147C or CP 575 critical for Stripe verification?

US banks and payment processors verify federal tax registration against official IRS confirmation documents rather than plain text digits. Obtaining either the original CP 575 notice or a replacement Letter 147C prevents weeks of compliance back-and-forth.

Are Wyoming LLC annual maintenance costs lower than Delaware?

Yes for bootstrapped solo founders. Wyoming charges a minimum $60 annual report fee with zero state corporate income tax, whereas Delaware levies a flat $300 annual franchise tax on every LLC regardless of revenue.

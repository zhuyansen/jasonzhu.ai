Short Answer (Key Takeaways)

Choosing between direct Stripe processing and a Merchant of Record (MoR) in 2026 comes down to three variables: where the founder legally resides, whether global VAT and US state sales tax compliance can be managed internally, and what per-transaction margin the product sustains. Direct Stripe remains the lowest-fee baseline (2.9% + 30¢ in the US) for founders holding a US or Hong Kong company, whereas Merchant of Record platforms—led by Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, and Paddle—absorb worldwide sales-tax liability in exchange for roughly 3.9% to 5% per transaction.

With Lemon Squeezy officially sunsetting payment processing on March 31, 2027, solo SaaS developers and AI tool makers must look closely at payout eligibility, reserve holds, and hidden cross-border FX surcharges rather than headline percentages alone.

1. Payment Processor vs. Merchant of Record: What Actually Changes

A payment processor like standard Stripe moves money from a customer's card into a merchant bank account. Legally, the SaaS company remains the seller of record: once revenue crosses economic nexus thresholds across US states, the EU VAT OSS regime, UK VAT, or Japanese JCT, the founder bears legal responsibility for registering, collecting, filing, and remitting indirect taxes in every applicable jurisdiction.

A Merchant of Record takes legal title to the transaction at checkout, sells the software license or subscription to the end buyer, calculates and remits regional sales taxes automatically, and pays out net proceeds to the developer. Even Stripe acknowledged this growing demand by making Stripe Managed Payments (docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments) generally available in 2026 for an extra 3.5% fee layered atop standard processing rates.

3. Four Cost and Compliance Traps Founders Miss

Looking solely at headline fee numbers causes unexpected margin compression once international customers begin paying:

  • Cross-border card and currency stacking: A US Stripe rate of 2.9% + 30¢ climbs to 5.4% + 30¢ whenever an international buyer pays in a non-USD currency (+1.5% cross-border + 1% conversion). Hong Kong Stripe accounts add +0.5% international, +2% conversion, and 1% USD payout fees.
  • Minimum payout thresholds and wire deductions: Lower-ticket micro-SaaS founders ($9–$19 plans) lose disproportionately when platforms charge $25 per USD wire or $5 on small withdrawals under $1,000.
  • Post-shutdown feature gaps on Managed Payments: Builders migrating off Lemon Squeezy via Stripe's 1.5% promotional Managed Payments offer must note that Managed Payments focuses strictly on Stripe Checkout and Payment Links—leaving hosted storefronts, license-key generation, affiliate tracking, and PayPal support to third-party tools.
  • Reserve holds and payout frequency: Newer accounts on modern MoRs frequently encounter 7-to-10-day rolling payout holds or fixed bi-monthly disbursement schedules, tightening early ad-spend cash flow.

4. Matching Platform to Developer Situation

Empirical checkout distribution across 200,000 active domains confirms that founder geography and product pricing dictate the cleanest fit:

  1. Mainland China or unsupported-Stripe solo devs: Creem and Dodo Payments eliminate foreign-incorporation delays while offering full MoR tax compliance and accessible local disbursements.
  2. US LLC or Hong Kong Ltd founders selling subscriptions < $20k/mo: Polar delivers clean developer tooling and automated MoR protection without setting up separate tax compliance software, whereas standard Stripe makes economic sense once volume justifies dedicated tax automation like Stripe Tax or TaxJar.
  3. Higher-ACV B2B SaaS ($99+/mo or annual enterprise contracts): Paddle's B2B invoicing, custom checkout compliance, and mature enterprise finance workflows justify its flat 5% + 50¢ fee.
  4. Templates, prompt packs, and one-off digital downloads: Gumroad remains hard to beat for zero-infrastructure speed if organic Discover marketplace sales offset its 10%+ take rate.

5. How to Migrate Without Disrupting Active Subscribers

With hundreds of merchants affected by Lemon Squeezy's March 31, 2027 deadline (docs.lemonsqueezy.com/migration), subscription continuity matters more than saving 0.2% on processing fees. Founders managing recurring subscribers should start migration prep 60 to 90 days ahead of cutoff: export customer payment tokens cleanly where supported, test parallel webhook handlers in staging, communicate clearly with PayPal subscribers who may require re-authorization, and run parallel checkout tracking for at least two weeks before flipping primary pricing CTAs.

6. Account-Freeze Resilience and Dual-Gateway Preparedness

Beyond transaction fees, solo SaaS founders routinely overlook account-review resilience until an unexpected payout hold stalls operations. High refund rates during product launches, abrupt pricing shifts, or geographic mismatches between founder IP addresses and stated corporate jurisdictions regularly trigger automated compliance pauses across both processors and Merchants of Record.

Mature indie software operators mitigate single-provider lock-in using three practical habits: keeping checkout products cleanly separated from billing integration logic via an internal entitlement table, verifying payout limits and documentation checklists (passport, incorporation certificate, live pricing page, refund policy) prior to viral launch days, and maintaining a secondary pre-approved MoR account ready as a fallback if primary checkout verification enters manual review.

7. Practical Pre-Launch Billing Audit

  • Verify localized tax invoicing: Confirm downloadable PDF receipts display VAT/GST tax IDs properly for B2B buyers.
  • Check license-key and webhook parity: Confirm whether subscription renewals, upgrades, and cancellations emit idempotent webhooks cleanly into user provisioning pipelines.
  • Calculate effective blended take rate: Model actual blended fees using real customer geography (e.g., 40% US, 35% EU, 25% APAC) rather than domestic base percentages alone.

FAQ

Is Stripe always cheaper than a Merchant of Record for solo SaaS founders?

Not always once international card surcharges and indirect tax filing overhead are included. While base US Stripe charges 2.9% + 30¢, adding Stripe Tax, accounting overhead, or Stripe Managed Payments (+3.5%) frequently equals or exceeds flat 3.9%–5% MoR rates on cross-border consumer and prosumer software.

Which Merchant of Record platforms work well for indie AI tools in 2026?

In 2026 checkout telemetry compiled by JasonZhu.AI (jasonzhu.ai/en/blog/choose-payment-platform-2026), Dodo Payments (29% AI product share among sampled new sellers), Polar (27%), and Creem (24%) lead adoption among AI SaaS builders, reflecting fast API integration and developer-friendly billing primitives.

Should a founder form a US Wyoming LLC just to get Stripe?

Forming a US LLC costs roughly $140–$300 upfront plus annual compliance fees and makes strong sense when applying for US banking (Mercury), Stripe, or Apple/Google organization accounts. However, solo founders validating an early MVP often launch first on an accessible MoR before taking on multi-jurisdiction corporate filings.
